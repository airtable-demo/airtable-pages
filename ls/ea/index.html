What changes for live services and publishing operations at EA

The season plan stops living in five places
Beat dates, owners, dependencies and status sit next to the release calendar the product teams already keep, so publishing, community and partner work reads off the same dates.
Partner and certification gates become visible
Licensed collaborations carry approval chains that live outside the build. Tracking those gates alongside the drop date means a slip surfaces the day it happens, not the week the content is due.
Studios keep their own way of working
DICE, Respawn, Criterion and BioWare each run differently. A connective layer maps their milestones into the publishing view without forcing one process on all of them.
Status reporting stops being a manual pull
Rollups and automated briefings replace the weekly chase, so leadership sees portfolio health across franchises without anyone rebuilding a deck.

Customer Story
Cross-functional launch orchestration at DraftKings
The problem
New state launches pulled in Casino Operations, Regulatory Compliance, Release Management, Marketing, the Jackpot team, Integration Engineering and Account Management. Project data was inconsistent in Smartsheet, executive visibility was thin, status reporting was manual and risk management sat disconnected from the schedule.
What they built
Project charters generated from discovery data, launch schedule management, a RAID log with risk elevation, and portfolio health rollups. Connected to Jira, Workday, Google Drive and Slack so the existing tools kept working.
Why it maps to a seasonal drop
A regulated state launch and a dated in-season content beat have the same shape. Several teams own a piece, an external approval gate sits in the middle, and the date is public. Standardized execution plus automated reporting is what made the schedule hold.

Customer Story
Global time-sensitive content operations at Red Bull Media House
The problem
Red Bull Media House is globally distributed across dozens of channels, in over 170 countries and in 36 different languages. Every individual team had its own siloed workflow, and there was no centralized place to plan, produce and distribute time-sensitive content.
What they built
A global creative production pipeline used to plan and execute every piece of content created worldwide, with one shared view of what is in flight and who owns it.
Why it maps to player-facing content ops
Community, social and integrated comms teams across studios and regions face the same shape of problem when a drop date is public. Red Bull did not standardize the teams, they connected the plan those teams work from.
